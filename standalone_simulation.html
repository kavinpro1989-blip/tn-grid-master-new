💡 Kavin's Top Questions & Answers (Tap to Review):

👩‍🏫 TEACHER QUESTION
Why must the grid frequency be locked at 50.00 Hz?

Answer: 50 Hz represents turbine rotational speed. If demand exceeds generation, generator turbines physically slow down. If frequency dips below 49.5 Hz, industrial motors overheat and safety switches trip to prevent transformer damage, causing blackouts.

👩‍🏫 TEACHER QUESTION
How did you calculate transmission line losses?

Answer: Joule's Law: P_loss = I² × R. Transmitting at 110 kV or 400 kV drops current (I), minimizing wire heat loss. Substations step it down to safe 230V AC for homes.

👨‍👩‍👧 PARENT QUESTION
What is the "Duck Curve" and how does the battery fix it?

Answer: At noon, solar creates a massive surplus "belly". At 6:30 PM, solar drops to zero right as everyone turns on ACs, creating a steep "neck". Our 485 MWh battery absorbs the midday belly and powers the evening neck!

👨‍👩‍👧 PARENT QUESTION
Why does home voltage drop on hot summer nights?

Answer: Thousands of neighborhood ACs pull huge current together, causing transformer voltage sag. Power drops from 230V to 180V, slowing ceiling fans until battery support steps in.

💡 Faaris's Top Questions & Answers (Tap to Review):

👩‍🏫 TEACHER QUESTION
Why can't Arduino power the fan directly without a relay?

Answer: An Arduino pin gives only 20–40mA. The fan motor pulls 150–200mA and creates back-EMF inductive spikes that would fry the chip. The relay acts as an isolated electromagnetic switch.

👩‍🏫 TEACHER QUESTION
How does the Arduino measure solar voltage safely?

Answer: Built-in 10-bit ADC on Pin A0 maps 0–5V DC into 0–1023. Code computes: Voltage = (val * 5.0) / 1023.0. Since panel is 4.9V, it stays safely within the 5.0V limit.
